# HOI4 Remade - Current Features

**Last Updated:** 2026-11-25

---

## TL;DR

**Map:** Clausewitz-style terrain, water, coasts, smooth mesh borders in HOI4 classes, rivers, country labels, haze, clouds, day/night with city lights, political / terrain / diplomatic map modes

**Units:** 1936 armies, on-map counters with action icons and battle bubbles, click and box selection, HOI4 movement rules, hourly movement, province-to-province combat with terrain, rivers and entrenchment, retreat and overrun, occupation, manpower pools, equipment stockpiles, reinforcement

**Armies and battle plans:** Armies with bottom-of-screen cards, front lines that divisions fill and hold, offensive plans (drag over targets) that advance as a broad front when executed, planning bonus

**Production:** Military factories from owned states, production lines in priority order with per-factory efficiency growth, commands for adding, sizing, reordering and deleting lines, AI that puts free factories to work, HOI4 production screen

**Construction:** Civilian factories and consumer goods from economy laws, a construction queue (civilian and military factories, infrastructure) with infrastructure speed-up and shared slots, construction AI and the construction screen

**Research:** The tech tree's technologies with each country's 1936 ones, research slots and speed, researching a tech in a slot at HOI4's rate (ahead-of-time penalty included, checked against a real game), empty slots saving up to 30 days, a research AI scoring techs with HOI4's own AI weights, the research screen's slots tab (no tech picker yet; finished techs unlock nothing yet)

**Recruit & deploy:** Training lines per deployment order take manpower up front, get equipment after the field, train up to their equipment level and deploy at the capital or a chosen province; recruitment AI and the recruit & deploy screen

**AI:** War AI in layers: theatres, budgets and 24-division armies holding front-line sections like the player's, odds-based attacks from the line; army plans (role templates, division mix, factory split); production, construction and recruitment AI

**Simulation:** Every decision is a command, one fixed-order hourly tick, fixed-point maths and adjacency-based fronts (deterministic, lockstep-ready), session recordings for debugging

**UI:** Top bar, production screen, research screen, state panel, division panel, army cards, battle plan window, diplomacy panel (UI Toolkit: UXML + USS + C#), all sprites loaded at runtime from the converted data

**Data:** One Python converter turns the HOI4 install into an Archon data directory

**War and peace:** War participation, surrender progress from victory points, capitulation, a basic peace conference (take states)

**Supply:** The 1936 railways and supply hubs, rail conversion after capture, each hub's rail connection to the capital and its capacity, railways drawn on the map; hub and state supply in every province and each division's supply ratio, and what low supply does to a division (combat, organisation, speed, reinforcement, attrition); the supply map mode (F4) and low supply crates on the counters

**Weather:** HOI4's weather per strategic region (rain, snow, blizzard, sandstorm), province temperatures, ground snow and mud, cutting supply and speed and attack and adding attrition

**Not Implemented:** strategic resources and trade, research, politics, generals, air and naval, factions, conditional surrender, saving/loading of HOI4-specific state, multiplayer in practice

The goal is to stress-test Archon-Engine, not to ship a game. Big visual wins are preferred over exact HOI4 fidelity.

## Map Rendering

- **HOI4 terrain surface** - 4x4 terrain atlas (diffuse+gloss, normal+spec) blended per map pixel from a terrain index map, tinted by the HOI4 colormap, lit by sun + ambient + Blinn-Phong specular
- **Heightmap-based coasts** - Smooth coast line from a height field instead of province pixels
- **Water** - Port of HOI4's water shader (LEAN waves, refracted sea floor, fresnel sky reflection, sun glint), calmer defaults
- **Political map mode** - Country colors with a soft gradient between countries; unowned land shows terrain
- **Terrain map mode** - Plain terrain
- **Diplomatic map mode** - Own country green, allies blue, enemies red, everyone else terrain (palette mode, opened by the diplomacy panel, refreshed on war and peace)
- **Mesh borders** - Smooth traced border meshes with HOI4 border textures
- **Border classes** - Sea, sea region, coast (no line, like HOI4), country, state, impassable, province
- **Rivers** - Traced and rendered with HOI4's river texture, width by river level, kept off water
- **Country labels** - Placed on the capital's territory piece, localised names, follow control
- **Atmosphere** - Haze and clouds
- **Day/night** - Sun cycle with city lights (HOI4 citylights texture, masked by the colormap)
- **Selection outline** - Smooth outline with glow for the selected province
- **State outline** - Second, lighter outline around the whole state of the selected province, drawn under the province outline
- **No hover highlight** - Disabled; HOI4 does not highlight provinces under the cursor
- **Visual style tuning** - `Tools/hoi4_visual_style.json5` is the master copy; hot reloaded from `Hoi4Data/map/visual_style.json5` in play mode

## Units

- **1936 armies** - Land divisions from HOI4's order of battle, with HOI4 division names; the game starts 1 January 1936
- **Division data** - Name, main type, organisation, strength, planning (all `FixedPoint64`) kept in the GAME layer, keyed by engine unit ID
- **Unit types** - One engine unit type per division main type
- **On-map counters** - One per country per province: plate in the country colour, type icon, flag, organisation and strength bars, division count, ideology marker, army colour strip. Composed into one atlas, drawn as one mesh, shrink and fade with zoom
- **Counter action icons** - Move arrow, defend shield or attack chevrons on the counter's right edge
- **Battle bubbles** - Marker on the border between the two provinces, coloured by whether the player is winning or losing
- **Selection** - Click a counter (Shift adds), left-drag box select for your own divisions, S selects half, click the map or press Escape to clear. Any country's counters can be inspected; only your own take orders
- **Move orders** - Right-click a province with own divisions selected: path and move
- **Halt** - H stops the selected divisions where they stand
- **Movement rules** - Land only, no water, no impassable states, owner must be own country, ally, enemy or military access; land crossings over straits (the Danish belts, Messina, Kerch...) and HOI4's impassable borders from `map/adjacencies.csv` (the engine's adjacency overrides)
- **Impassable land** - Nobody can enter it, so (as in HOI4) an impassable state goes to whoever controls most of its border: daily, when the country holding the longest stretch of it (pixel edges with passable land, `map/hoi4_impassable_borders.csv`) is at war with its controller, all its provinces pass to that country (a third country liberates it for the owner)
- **Move arrows** - HOI4's `move_thick` arrow ribbon along the route (centripetal Catmull-Rom), travelled part in a lighter colour, red for attacks
- **Hourly movement** - Divisions advance every game hour, so progress and arrows fill smoothly
- **Movement time** - Each leg takes km between the province centres x the destination terrain's movement_cost / the division's speed (its slowest battalion), times its modifiers: its own terrain movement modifier there, a river crossing (-25% small, -50% large, plus its river movement modifier) and the destination's infrastructure (-5% per level below 5). Checked against daily division snapshots of a real 1936 game (unopposed infantry: about 4 km of distance x cost an hour). Province centres in `map/hoi4_province_centres.csv`
- **Organisation on the move** - Moving divisions lose 0.2 organisation an hour on top of their recovery (HOURLY_ORG_MOVEMENT_IMPACT), so a march leaves them just under full, as in real HOI4 snapshots; taking an enemy province costs a fifth of max organisation (ORG_LOSS_FACTOR_ON_CONQUER); below 30% organisation a move into enemy land slows, down to -80% at none (LOW_ORG_FOR_ATTACK, ZERO_ORG_MOVEMENT_MODIFIER)
- **Combat** - Province-to-province battles across a border, from converted HOI4 equipment stats (attack, defence, breakthrough, hardness, armour, piercing) and combat width with reserves. The attack starts when the move starts; the move into the province is held while an enemy is there. No counter-attack into the province you are being attacked from
- **Terrain in combat** - The target's terrain (HOI4 `00_terrain.txt`) sets the combat width (plains 70, forest 60, mountain 50, urban 80...), plus its support width for each extra attack direction (flanking), and the attacker's attack and breakthrough penalty (forest -15% up to mountain -50%)
- **River crossings** - Attacking across a river costs the divisions crossing it 30% (small) or 60% (large); crossings come from HOI4 `rivers.bmp` along province borders
- **Strait crossings in combat** - Attacking over a strait is amphibious (HOI4's "Naval Penalty"): -50% (AMPHIBIOUS_LANDING_PENALTY) plus the division's own amphibious attack (its line battalions' `amphibious` attack averaged, plus its support companies' in full: a 1936 Panzer-Division gets -18.3%, -68.33% in all, as in a live HOI4 battle), on attack and breakthrough
- **Battalion terrain modifiers** - Each battalion's HOI4 terrain blocks (forest, hills, mountain, jungle, marsh, plains, urban, desert, river, amphibious, fort), combined per division like HOI4 (line battalions averaged, support companies added): attackers get their attack modifier in the target's terrain (added to the terrain's own penalty) and their river attack when crossing one, defenders their defence modifier in the terrain and their river defence when attacked over a river. movement modifiers slow or speed its movement (see Movement time); forts aren't modelled
- **Entrenchment** - A division standing still out of battle digs in 1 level a day, up to 5; each level gives a defender +2% attack and defence; lost on moving or attacking. Shown in the division panel
- **Manpower** - Each country's pool is the engine's `ResourceSystem` resource `manpower` (saved with the game): recruitable manpower (core population + 2% of non-core, times the mobilisation law) minus the men in its divisions, growing 1.5% of recruitable a year
- **Equipment** - Divisions track equipment per archetype (infantry, support, artillery, trucks, light tanks): what their sub-units `need` and what they have, starting at `start_equipment_factor`. A sub-unit is as strong as its scarcest equipment; strength is capped by equipment and by men. Battle losses take the men and 70% of the equipment (`EQUIPMENT_COMBAT_LOSS_FACTOR`)
- **Stockpiles** - One engine `ResourceSystem` resource per archetype per country, starting at the history's `add_equipment_to_stockpile` (nearly empty, as in real HOI4). Production adds to them, reinforcement takes from them. Weekly notes per country (made, delivered, stock)
- **Equipment versions** - Every stockpile and every division's equipment of an archetype keeps which versions it is made of (shares of the amount, newest first: losses and attrition take every version alike). 1936 divisions and stockpiles hold their country's newest buildable version (a real 1936 German division holds infantry equipment I, artillery I, support equipment I; Argentina, which only has `infantry_weapons` in 1936, holds infantry equipment 0), production adds its line's version, deliveries hand out the newest first. Upgrades: a division with all it needs of an archetype but older versions than the stockpile's newest swaps up to 10% of its need a day, by priority, and the old equipment goes back to the stockpile. Disbanding, cancelled training and smaller templates return equipment with its versions. Weekly `versions` notes (upgraded, and what the field holds where versions mix)
- **Division stats from equipment** - Soft and hard attack, defence, breakthrough, hardness, armour, piercing and reliability come from the versions a division holds, weighted by amount: per sub-unit its equipment's stats x (1 + its own factor), the highest hardness, armour and piercing; per division the sums, averages and the 30/70 (armour) and 40/60 (piercing) best/average split. With the 1936 designs this gives exactly the old template numbers; on each country's own versions 82 of 212 templates lose attack (minors on infantry equipment 0). Archetypes no catalogue version covers (special projects) keep their 1936 design's stats. Not yet: speed by version, the designer still shows the 1936 designs, the template's equipment choice
- **Reinforcement** - Daily, every division short of equipment wants up to 10% of its need per archetype from the stockpile, shared with the training lines by priority (equal shares within one priority; see Recruit and Deploy), and every division short of men up to 10% of its manpower from the pool; then strength rises to what equipment and men allow. Countries and divisions in id order. Readiness checks (AI, offensives) measure against that full strength
- **Combat factors** - Planning, terrain and river (attackers) and entrenchment (defenders) multiply per division, like HOI4; the AI's odds use the same factors
- **Retreat and overrun** - Broken defenders march to a friendly neighbouring province (a day); a retreater that arrives where an enemy stands is destroyed. Broken attackers drop their order
- **Occupation** - Entering a province held by an enemy flips its controller (not owner), unless an enemy division stands there (only a retreat arrives so, and it is overrun); daily, a division standing still out of battle on an enemy's land with no enemy there takes it (`occupation/settle` notes); occupied land is drawn in the occupier's colour, lakes follow the land around them; land of a country the capturer isn't at war with (an ally's, or a third country's an enemy occupied) goes back to its owner. At a peace, land held by a country not at war with its owner goes back to the owner, and divisions left on land that changed hands without access go home. Daily, any division on land held by a country it isn't at war with and whose owner gives it no access (allied, military access) is placed in the nearest province its country controls (`Hoi4ReturnHome`; start garrisons abroad without modelled access, like Japan's in Manchukuo, go home on day one; `return_home` notes)
- **Hover blocking** - No province hover or click through counters or panels

## Production

`Assets/Game/Scripts/Production/` (wiki "Production", `NDefines.NProduction`):

- **Military factories** - A state's `arms_factory` buildings count for its owner (the country owning most of its provinces) while the owner controls more than half of them; recounted when ownership or control changes, so conquest and peace deals move factories. Day one matches real HOI4 (Germany 28)
- **Production lines** - Per country, in priority order, starting from the 1936 lines in the OOB. Each day the lines take factories top-down (requested, at most 150 per line) until the country's factories run out; a lost factory comes off the bottom and its slot loses 1% a day down to the start efficiency while unused (it resumes from there), an added one starts at the start efficiency (10% + `production_factory_start_efficiency_factor` as points: +5 per dispersed industry)
- **Output** - 4.5 IC per active factory x its efficiency x (1 + `industrial_capacity_factory`: laws, ideas, concentrated/dispersed industry) a day; whole units come out when the line's progress reaches the equipment's `build_cost_ic`, the rest carries over, and go into the stockpile
- **Efficiency** - Per factory slot, growing by 0.001 x cap² / current x (1 + `production_factory_efficiency_gain_factor`) a day up to the cap, 50% + `production_factory_max_efficiency_factor` as points (+10 per machine tools technology; about 500 days from nothing); a slot above the cap stays. The country's rates follow its ideas and researched technologies (`ProductionRates`). Rules measured on a real German game's daily lines (`Tools/reference/hoi4_production_modifiers.py`: cap 30 -> 40 at basic machine tools on Hard, start 10 -> 15 at dispersed industry, output +0.10 per dispersed industry). Stability adds its factory output bonus or penalty (see Modifiers). Not yet: MIO bonuses, dynamic modifiers, building slot factors
- **First day** - Until the first game day has passed, factories move freely between the 1936 lines without losing efficiency (added ones start at the line's starting efficiency); a line made that day starts at 10% like any new line
- **Buildable equipment** - Per country and archetype, the newest equipment its technologies unlock (a tank design comes with its chassis technology: the chassis' `derived_variant_name`, else the same name with `_equipment_`, else for the tier-0 chassis the Great War tank). Special project equipment (helicopters, land cruisers, super-heavy artillery, the armoured support vehicle: `common/special_projects`, researched in facilities) is never buildable. At the start from its 1936 technologies (no-DLC history branches skipped) (a 1936 line's equipment counts when nothing newer is known), then each finished technology's `enable_equipments` replaces it when newer (year, then cost), from the equipment catalogue `common/hoi4_equipment.json5` (76 land equipment: archetype, parent, year, cost, combat stats, names and pictures per country). Lines keep their equipment until switched; `unlock` notes
- **Switching equipment** - A line switches to the country's current equipment of an archetype and keeps efficiency x retention per slot, at least the start efficiency; the unit in progress is lost. Retention: 30% for a direct upgrade or downgrade (parent and child), 20% within the archetype otherwise, 70% for another model of the same tank chassis, 10% for anything else, raised by `line_change_production_efficiency_factor` (dispersed industry, flexible line) as RET + (1 - RET) x bonus. Checked on a real German game's line switches (variant 0.9 + 0.1 x 0.2 = 0.92; a 30% switch landed on the start efficiency, 27.5%). `switch` notes
- **Commands** - Add a line (bottom of the queue), set its factories, move it in the queue, delete it, switch its equipment, all through `TryExecuteCommand`
- **Production AI** - Its lines switch to newer equipment as research unlocks it. Weekly, each country but the player's on its own weekday: free factories go one at a time to the archetype with the most need per factory already making it (what its divisions lack beyond the stockpile, plus a 10% reserve of their total use); what lacks counts divisions in training as well as the field; with an army plan the reserve follows the plan's factory split (none once the stockpile holds a quarter of the army's use), lines of archetypes outside the plan are given up once nothing lacks them, and up to two factories a week move from lines over the plan, or whose stockpile is past that ceiling with nothing lacking (a country short of men piles up rifles), to archetypes under the plan or the planned one least stocked. The plan's split follows what recruitment will train (each role's gap to its share), not the nominal mix. Host only, through the same commands
- **Production screen** - HOI4's `countryproductionlineview.gui` layout and art, opened by the top bar's production button (glows while factories are free) or Y. Resource strip (the country's own states), efficiency cap, military factories used/total, add-line buttons for infantry/artillery and armour opening the equipment picker (design name, general name, cost, picture), category filters with factory counts. Each line: priority, design name, efficiency bar relative to the cap, +/- factories (Shift x5, Ctrl x10, or the x5/x10 buttons), delete, priority arrows, picture, units a day or week with progress to the next one, what the divisions lack (reinforcing) or the stock (stockpiling), the 5x3 factory grid (x5/x10 icons past 15, grey where the line wants more than it gets) glowing while factories are free. A new line asks for the free factories, 1 to 15; each line shows its own equipment, and clicking its picture opens the picker with what it can switch to and the efficiency it keeps
- **Recording** - Weekly `production` notes (factories, free, each line's factories, efficiency, IC), `ai_assign` notes, a Production section in `report.py`, `production` and `ai_production` columns in `perf.tsv`

## Construction

`Assets/Game/Scripts/Construction/` (wiki "Construction", `NDefines.NProduction` / `NBuildings`):

- **Buildings** - `Hoi4Buildings`: state building levels as they are now (the 1936 history plus what was built), and the country each state's buildings work for (the owner holding most provinces while it controls more than half). Production counts military factories from here; the state panel shows it
- **Civilian factories and consumer goods** - Consumer goods take floor((civilian + military) x economy law share x (1 + consumer goods factor)), at least 10% and 1; the rest are available. Laws, factors and construction speed (overall and per building) come from the country's modifiers (see Modifiers): its ideas (default laws filled in) and researched technologies, so they change as it researches. Matches real HOI4 Germany (15 of 63, 20 available)
- **Queue** - Per country, in order; an order holds one or more levels of a building in a state (queueing it there again adds a level) and builds them one at a time; each order takes up to 15 available civilian factories at 5 IC a day x (1 + construction speed + the building's speed) x (1 + infrastructure / 5). Civilian factory 10800, military factory 7200, infrastructure 6000 (and per-level costs where HOI4 has them). Finished levels go to the state; new military factories reach production the same day
- **Buildings** - Civilian and military factories, infrastructure, air bases, anti-air, radar, dockyards (coastal states only), synthetic refineries and fuel silos (no effect yet beyond their levels). Technology-gated ones (anti-air, radar, refinery, fuel silo) need the country's researched technologies (live, from `Hoi4Research`), up to the level they unlock; locked ones are greyed out on the construction screen
- **Limits** - Factories need a free shared slot (state category plus extra slots from state history, at most 25, counting the queue); state buildings up to their cap (infrastructure 5, air base 10, anti-air 5, radar 6), air bases lower on islands (6/4/2); only states whose buildings work for the country; items in a state the country loses are dropped
- **Commands** - Queue levels, set an order's levels, remove, move in the queue
- **Construction screen** - Top bar button or T (shares the left side with production): civilian factories in use, owned, construction speed, consumer goods, one row per order (levels +/-, factories out of 15 over a box that animates faster with more factories, progress, priority arrows, delete), building buttons per section in HOI4's order; with one selected a province click queues it in that state, and the map shows where it fits: plain terrain, the player's states green with room and blue when full, yellow bands over states with an order that leaves room and blue bands where the orders fill the state, a soft green glow around the state under the cursor, state names when zoomed in to unit level (Archon region labels), no borders between a state's provinces while placing state-wide buildings, a box per state with the building's levels (built+queued/most), infrastructure speed bonus and shared slots in use (prototype `Tools/prototypes/construction/construction_map.py`)
- **Construction AI** - Weekly per country but the player's: keeps enough items queued for all available civilian factories (15 each, plus one): civilian factories until 1938, military factories from then or at war, in the state with a free slot and the best infrastructure; infrastructure when no slot is left
- **Recording** - Weekly `construction` notes (civilian, military, consumer goods, free, queue, built), `done`, `dropped` and `ai_queue` notes, a Construction section in `report.py`, `construction` and `ai_construction` columns in `perf.tsv`

## Modifiers

`Assets/Game/Scripts/Modifiers/Hoi4Modifiers.cs` on the engine's `ModifierSystem` (`GameState.Modifiers`):

- **Sources** - Per country, its ideas and laws (`common/hoi4_ideas.json5`, listed per country in `hoi4_country_economy.json5`; default laws filled in, an idea listed twice counted once, no-DLC branches skipped since every DLC is assumed owned) as Policy sources, and its researched technologies (their `modifiers`) as Technology sources, added at the start and as each one finishes
- **Keys** - Only the keys the game reads get an engine type id (HOI4 has 625 across ideas and technologies, the engine 512 a scope): consumer goods share and factor, construction speed and every per-building construction speed, research speed, factory output, efficiency cap, start and growth. Ids in sorted order, the same on every machine
- **Values** - A key's values summed over the country's sources (HOI4's rule), except `consumer_goods_factor`, which multiplies (product of 1 + value). `Changed` fires with the country when its modifiers change; research rebooks on a speed change
- **Stability** - `Hoi4Stability`: `set_stability` + the country's `stability_factor` (ideas, landmarks), 0..1, shown live in the top bar. Above 50% `stability_good_modifier` applies scaled by (stability - 0.5) / 0.5 (factory output up to +20%, consumer goods factor up to -20%), below 50% `stability_bad_modifier` by (0.5 - stability) / 0.5 (output down to -50%); `common/hoi4_static_modifiers.json5`. Checked on a real German game: output moves 0.4 per point of stability, and day one's consumer goods (15 of 63) need the full scaled -20% beside Germany's own +10% dynamic modifier (not modelled, so ours gives 14). Not yet: stability changing (events, focuses, decisions), war support's stability penalty in war, political power
- **Landmarks** - `common/hoi4_landmarks.json5` (16 from state history): a landmark's country modifiers (mostly +5% stability: Reichstag, Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty...) go to its province's controller while it is on the landmark's list, rechecked when control changes, so taking Berlin costs Germany its 5%
- **Python** - `Tools/hoi4_modifiers.py` sums a country's start modifiers the same way (landmarks and stability included), for prototypes

## Research

`Assets/Game/Scripts/Research/` (wiki "Research", `NDefines.NCountry` / `NTechnology`):

- **Slots** - `set_research_slots` from the country's 1936 history, else `BASE_RESEARCH_SLOTS` (2), in `common/hoi4_country_economy.json5`. Matches real HOI4 Germany (4)
- **Research speed** - `research_speed_factor` of the country's modifiers (ideas and researched technologies; Germany +1% at the start, as in HOI4); a finished tech that changes it rebooks the slots
- **Technologies** - `common/hoi4_technologies.json5` (converter `write_technologies`): the tech tree folders' technologies (328; doctrines are a separate system and not in), every DLC assumed owned (the no-DLC folders and branches left out), with name, cost, start year, folder and grid position, paths (with their cost coefficient), dependencies, exclusions, what they enable and their modifiers; each country's 1936 technologies from its history
- **Researching** - A tech needs research_cost x 110 points (`BASE_TECH_COST`). A slot gains each day max(0.1, 1 + research speed) / (1 + 2 x years before 1 January of its start year), recomputed daily, so research ahead of time speeds up as the year nears. It can start once any one tech leading to it is known (a root always), every dependency is known and nothing it excludes is. Progress stays with a tech that leaves its slot. A finished tech is known and adds its research speed. Checked against a real 1936 German game's daily save blocks (prototype `Tools/prototypes/research/research_cost.py`: finished techs' points, the start factors, the daily penalty, the any-parent rule)
- **Saved research** - Each day an empty slot saves a day of research, up to `BASE_RESEARCH_POINTS_SAVED` (30); picking a tech in it spends them at once at the slot's rate
- **Cost** - Nothing runs per slot per day: when a tech goes into a slot, or the country's speed changes, its finish day is found by summing the coming days' rates and booked; a day only finishes the slots booked for it. Progress is summed on demand (the research screen's one country) and saved days are the days since the slot emptied: the same sums as a daily tick. The AI compiles the HOI4 triggers once into typed nodes
- **Commands** - `SetResearchCommand` (a tech in a slot, or -1 to empty it)
- **Research AI** - `Hoi4ResearchAI`, on the host, each country but the player's on its own day of a 28-day cycle (waiting costs nothing: an empty slot saves up to 30 days and the pick spends them) when it has an empty slot. A tech excluded by one in another slot is not picked. Score: the tech's `ai_will_do` (base, then the modifiers whose triggers hold: AND/OR/NOT, date, tag, is_major, has_war, has_tech, factory counts, DLC owned; others left out) x active `common/ai_strategy` research weights (research_tech ones picked first), none past the AI's ahead-of-time limit (penalty 3), / (1 + 4 x the penalty), x 60 / (60 + days to research). Pick at random among the techs scoring 75% of the best (NAI defines). Majors: the 7 with most factories plus any over 0.7 x their average. In two recorded HOI4 games the AI's real picks fall in that band 78-79% of the time, a random tech's chance 3-4%; by 15 February 1937 AI countries have 4.65 techs on average, the real AI 5.06 (prototype `Tools/prototypes/research/research_ai.py`)
- **Research screen** - HOI4's `countrytechnologyview.gui` layout and art, opened by the top bar's research button or W (shares the left side with production, construction and deployment): limited bonuses, research speed, one entry per slot; an empty slot glows (the two glow frames blended) and its bar shows the saved research out of 30 days; a researching slot shows the tech's picture, its start year and the days left. Clicking a slot opens the tech tree for it. The Experimental Facilities tab is a placeholder (prototype `Tools/prototypes/research/render_research.py`)
- **Tech tree** - HOI4's `countrytechtreeview.gui` folders (infantry, support, armour, artillery, naval, naval support, air, electronics, industry) with their tabs, backgrounds, year and section labels. Each tech sits on the gridbox of its first ancestor that has one (format LEFT: y across, x down; other formats start the tree at its leftmost column), and right-anchored year labels follow the visible width; HOI4's own scrollbars (`Hoi4Scrollbar`, on every list: tech tree, research, production, construction, deployment, designer, division panel, peace conference, confirm dialog); techs unlocking equipment are large cards with the country's short design name (GER "ALZ 13"), the rest small icons, with the country's own picture where it has one. Backgrounds by state (researched, available, researching as HOI4's sliding chevron strip, locked); links through slot centres coloured by the child's state. Clicking an available tech puts it in the slot the tree was opened from (`SetResearchCommand`). The converter hides branches reached only through hidden no-DLC techs (special forces) (prototype `Tools/prototypes/research/render_techtree.py`)
- **Not yet** - Tech tooltips, sub-technology slots, what finished techs unlock in sub-units (equipment they unlock goes to production, then to divisions as upgrades), their modifiers beyond construction and research speed, limited bonuses, design teams, doctrines, experimental facilities
- **Recording** - `research` notes (`start`, `done`), `research_ai` `pick` notes (the pick, the band, the best), `research` and `ai_research` columns in `perf.tsv`

## Recruit and Deploy

`Assets/Game/Scripts/Deployment/` (`NDefines.NDeployment` / `NMilitary`, `common/units` training_time; no wiki page, real 1936 German training lines in the reference workspace's `deployment.tsv` and country blocks):

- **Orders and lines** - Per country: deployment orders of a template (deploy province: none at first, HOI4's red "No location set", deploying at the capital then; the player picks a state land-connected to the capital, shown in green bands; priority low/normal/high, series amount) with training lines (Add Unit adds one). Each line trains one division at a time and, once it deploys, the next, until it has made the order's amount; infinite (HOI4's default) keeps training. Commands for new orders, more lines, the amount, cancelling a line or order, priority, location, order up/down and deploying early
- **Manpower** - A line takes the template's whole manpower when it starts (as HOI4: 9,600 men from day one); it waits while the pool is short or the men in training would pass max(100,000, 75% of the men in the field). Fewer divisions or a cancelled order give men and equipment back
- **Equipment** - Field divisions (reinforcing) and started lines share the stockpile each day: each wants up to 10% of its need of an archetype; higher priority first (the country's reinforcement priority for the field, each order's for its lines, all normal by default), within that field divisions by their template's priority (elite, normal, reserve: 1936 history has 27 reserve and 8 elite templates), equal shares of what they want within one tier, as HOI4 splits equipment between reinforcing and training
- **Training** - One day a day of the template's training time (its slowest sub-unit: infantry 90 days, support 120, armour 180), never above the line's equipment level (HOI4's max_training follows equipment), so lines wait for the factories
- **Deploying** - Divisions deploy with experience in proportion to their training, up to level 1 (DEPLOY_TRAINING_MAX_LEVEL): fully trained Trained, early Green; the division list and the recruit rows show the level (green, trained, regular, seasoned, veteran from UNIT_EXP_LEVELS; 1936 divisions from their history). Fully trained lines deploy on their own and start the next division of their series (new men and equipment, no training), or go when the series is done; early from 20% training (MIN_DIVISION_DEPLOYMENT_TRAINING) with the equipment they have. New divisions are named from the template's names group, numbered on from the 1936 divisions (Germany's next is 26, as in HOI4), at the order's province, or the capital when the country lost it
- **Recruitment AI** - Weekly per country but the player's: trains what manpower and equipment allow, keeping a bank of 10% of the deployed army's men and equipment use (a new line only when the pool after the waiting lines, and the stockpile of every archetype the template needs after what the field and lines lack, stay above it), at most 10 new lines a week, deploying at the capital; by the army plan's role shares of the army, field and training (the role furthest under its share first, the next when it can't be afforded; other templates' divisions count by kind), each line training one division so every new one is chosen again; before the first plan its most common template
- **Army AI** (`Hoi4ArmyAI`, prototype `Tools/prototypes/ai_templates/design_templates.py`) - Each AI country keeps one template per role, changed in place (no copies or decommissioning, unlike HOI4's AI): defence infantry (engineers and support artillery always), offensive infantry (4+ military factories), mobile (motorized with 5+, else cavalry with no vehicles in it), tanks with motorized infantry (8+). First review in the first week, then every 90 days, at most 3 reviews a day (a queue, countries without a plan and then the biggest military first; design searches are shared between countries with the same start, options and equipment costs): the templates are sorted into roles (defence: the infantry template holding the most frontage), each role is designed by hill climbing over single edits scored from the designer sums per combat width (diminishing returns, less equipment IC and manpower, an organisation floor per role), and production is planned (factories by the IC the 70/18/8/4 mix needs, after a player's Germany of 1939: 88% infantry, 8% motorized, 4% tanks; an archetype under half a factory drops out and the roles are designed again without it). A role template is saved when 5% better; other templates train no more and are deleted once empty. Recorded as `army` notes (plan, design)
- **Recruit & deploy screen** - Top bar button right of production, or U (shares the left side with production and construction). Equipment priority rows with red/yellow/green low/normal/high dots: reinforcements is real (field against training) and shows the field's equipment, the others are shown, not changeable, then one block per order: template icon, name, -/+ the series amount (infinity icon by default, - off there; + sets 1, then counts up, Shift: 5; - from 1 back to infinite), low/normal/high priority, deploy location (click, then a province you hold), Add Unit, deploy all, cancel; a summary row (move the order up or down, mean equipment, divisions, collapse) and a row per line (its division's name, equipment and training, yellow while stalled, its place in the series n/N or n/infinite, deploy early from 20%, cancel). Division templates window beside it: special forces cap (max(24, 5% of battalions)), templates with icons, reserve/elite chevrons (click to cycle; also on the order icons and in the division list), Train (an order of one), Edit (the division designer), Delete (with HOI4's confirmation, only while no division uses the template, in the field or in training) and the Division Designer button (a new template); decommissioning greyed out (prototype `Tools/prototypes/deployment/render_deployment.py`)
- **Recording** - Weekly `deployment` notes (lines, started, mean training and equipment, waiting for manpower, pool and cap), `order`, `deployed`, `cancel` and `ai_train` notes, a Deployment section in `report.py`, `deployment` and `ai_deployment` columns in `perf.tsv`

## Division Designer

`Assets/Game/Scripts/UI/Hoi4DivisionDesigner.cs`, `Units/Hoi4TemplateDesign.cs` (`interface/divisiondesignerview.gui`, `NDefines.NMilitary` designer defines, `common/units` sub-units, `common/technologies` enable_subunits; prototype `Tools/prototypes/designer/render_designer.py`):

- **Sub-units** - Every land battalion and support company with the designer's stats (recovery, recon, suppression, weight, supply use, reliability, air attack, entrenchment, initiative) and display name. Per country the ones it can use in 1936: unlocked by a technology it has (or not gated by any and active), and every equipment archetype it needs buildable or stockpiled (France 24, Ethiopia 9)
- **Grid** - Templates keep HOI4's brigade positions. 5 columns of up to 4 battalions (the fifth row needs a doctrine, MIN_DIVISION_BRIGADE_HEIGHT), filled in order (one open slot under a column's last battalion and in the next column, then locks), one sub-unit group per brigade; 5 divisional support companies, one of each type (not `divisional = no`); regimental support: one company under each brigade of 3 battalions (REGIMENTAL_SUPPORT_REQUIRED_BATTALIONS) whose `allowed_battalion_groups` take the brigade's group (infantry guns, fire support, AA/AT batteries, SP AA and tank destroyer support), half a company's cost; 1936's one regimental company (CZE) is read from the history. Removing closes the gaps
- **Stats** - `Hoi4Divisions.Stats`, shared with the divisions: sums (HP, attacks, defence, breakthrough, suppression, weight, supply, manpower, equipment), means over every sub-unit (organisation, recovery, reliability, initiative, hardness), slowest speed of the sub-units that affect it (not `affects_speed = no`, never regimental support: engineers no longer slow a motorized division), battalion combat width; regimental support counts in full otherwise (HOI4's scaling with brigade size isn't in the data), armour/piercing 30/40% best plus 70/60% mean, cut to one decimal as HOI4 shows them; matches HOI4's designer for France's Division Coloniale
- **Saving** - `SaveTemplateCommand`: a new template or over one (renaming it). Its divisions in the field and in training take the new layout; equipment and men past the new need go back to the stockpile and the pool, strength and organisation keep their share of the new maximum; orders follow a rename. Main type (icon, counter, unit type) from the highest-priority battalion
- **Screen** - HOI4's window over the map: template icon, name (typed), name list, reserve/normal/elite, the composition summary (hardness, organisation, HP, speed, combat width), the grid and support column (+ on an empty brigade opens the categories, HOI4's sub-unit groups with "New group" for one the template lacks, then that group's battalions; a used brigade goes straight to its group; "Cost N to add/replace" green, or red without the experience; right click removes), the regimental support row (locked), base/combat/equipment sheets, Reset, Duplicate (an unsaved copy under a free name: closing drops it), Save, the experience the changes would cost (5 a battalion, 10 a company, 20 a new brigade group; shown, not charged)

## Armies and Battle Plans

- **Armies** - Named groups of divisions with a fixed colour each. Made from the selection with the plus card, right-click a card to add the selection, cross to disband; an army with no divisions disappears. No general or size limit yet
- **Army cards** - Bottom-of-screen row (HOI4 leader groups): left-click selects the army's divisions, play / stop buttons above armies with a front line, animated chevrons while the plan runs
- **Battle plan window** - Shows while at least one selected division is in an army: front line tool, offensive tool, assign, unassign, delete tool. Assign: click a front line and the selected divisions join it, and its army if it is another one's; click an offensive and they join its line and stay on the front the offensive belongs to. Unassign: the selected divisions leave their lines at once, stop, and stay off lines until assigned again. The bin turns the delete tool on: unit counters hide, the line or offensive under the cursor turns red and a click removes it; Escape, right click or the bin again turns it off. Shift + right click on the bin removes every order of the selected army at once (all its front lines and offensives)
- **Front lines** - Hover a country to preview the border as a green dashed line with striped own provinces, click to place it in the army colour. A line covers only the stretch of border nearest the cursor: where a third country splits the border (Luxembourg between Belgium and Alsace) each side is its own line, and stretches that join as the border moves become one. Hold the right button and drag along the border to draw only part of a stretch (HOI4: the line in the army colour under the dashed preview line, a ring at each end); the partial line follows the border without creeping along it. Lines are split into separate fronts per enemy pocket and own pocket, traced again whenever provinces change hands, removed when the country no longer borders you
- **Holding the line** - Assigned divisions spread evenly over the front provinces and return to their spots; a division standing on a front province of its line keeps that one (an attacker that took a province stays in it, not back to its old spot), else keeps its spot while that province is still on its front and the front has room, so a province won or lost moves a division or two, not the whole line; a division that already held a spot takes a new one only within 500 km (else it doubles up nearby), so a long line doesn't send divisions across the continent when its fronts change length; an AI division sent to attack keeps its spot and line, and comes back to it; new army divisions join the least-loaded plan automatically; a manual order takes a division off its plan until it stops, then it goes back
- **Line labels** - "N Divs, army name" bent letter by letter along each front, scaled and hidden with the counters' zoom rules
- **Offensive plans** - With the offensive tool, enemy provinces facing the army's lines stripe yellow; right-drag picks targets (green, HOI4 magnet trail and dotted line), release places an arrow from the front into the targets plus an objective line on their far side. Click picks one province; Tab switches which front the arrow starts from; live arrow preview while placing; several offensives per army, unassigned ones drawn with HOI4's unassigned masks
- **Executing plans** - The play button sends the offensive's divisions across the line into the zone between the front and the targets, spreading the push over the zone's width. The line is retraced after each capture, so it advances as a front. Divisions attack only when rested enough and break off when worn down; the army stops once every target is taken
- **Planning bonus** - Divisions on the start line of an offensive build planning daily (blue bar in the division list), decaying while moving, fighting or on manual orders; it raises attack and breakthrough
- **Plans turn green under the cursor**
- **Simulation vs presentation** - Fronts come from the province adjacency graph with integer maths; the drawn lines and arrows (`Hoi4/PlanLine`, `Hoi4/PlanArrow`, `_Hoi4PlanTex` stripes) are presentation only, for the player's plans

## War and Peace

- **War participation** - Per country against each enemy (HOI4 defines): 0.1 per strength point of damage dealt, 0.5 per 1,000 casualties inflicted, and per province taken 0.2 per victory point plus 0.2 x its share of the state's value. A winner's participation is its share of its side's points
- **Surrender progress** - The share of the victory points in a country's owned core territory its enemies hold, over the 80% surrender limit (colonies and annexed land don't count: France falls with France; a country owning no cores counts all its land against 80% x 0.7; provinces when it has no victory points). Shown in the diplomacy panel for both sides of a war
- **Capitulation** - At 100%, once the war is 7 days old: the loser's divisions disband, its land not yet held by a winner goes to the winner it capitulates to (the one holding its capital, else the one with the most participation), and a peace conference opens among everyone at war with it. The conference hides the rest of the UI and the unit counters until it ends
- **After the peace** - Divisions left on land their country may not enter (no war, alliance or military access) go to their capital
- **Peace conference** - Basic HOI4 version, checked against a live conference (`Tools/prototypes/peace/`): score = 1.35 x the value of the loser's states the winners control, shared by participation. Take State only, one turn, no contests. Cost = state value (10 + population / 100,000 + resources + 4 x category slots + building values) x ideology (democratic 1.3, fascist 0.9) x 0.8 when the taker controls it (x 10 when no winner does) x influence (0.7 to 1.0 by distance to the taker's capital, cores and controlled states, cheaper next to states already demanded). With the player among the winners the game pauses on the conference screen (HOI4 peaceconferencewindow.gui layout and art): Make Demands on the left (Select All, Reset), the victors on the right, cost cards on the map (click a state to demand it), the loser's states in its own colour (occupation hidden) with HOI4's broad green stripes, blue once demanded, Confirm and Exit. The AI winners then take the states they control, best value for the cost first. Taken states change owner, the rest of the occupation ends, the wars end
- **Command** - `PeaceDealCommand` carries the player's demands

## Supply

`Hoi4Railways` (`Assets/Game/Scripts/Supply/`), checked first in `Tools/prototypes/supply/rail_network.py`. No graphics yet. Built on two engine mechanisms: `ProvinceLinkNetwork` (the rail links and the widest path) and `ProvinceSystem.TryGetControlChangeTick` (conversion time).

- **Railways** - HOI4's 3,322 rail links between bordering provinces (`map/railways.txt`; a link on several railways takes the highest level), levels 1 to 5 (1,544 at level 1, 1,658 at 2, 89 at 3, 31 at 4 in 1936)
- **Supply hubs** - The 727 hubs of `map/supply_nodes.txt`, plus every country's capital (a capital is always a hub; 152 capitals aren't in the file, mostly tags that don't exist in 1936)
- **Conversion** - Rail in a province that changes controller can't be used by the new controller for 10 days, 5 when the state is its core (`RAILWAY_CONVERSION_COOLDOWN`, `_CORE`). The hub itself needs none, but its province's rail does
- **Connection to the capital** - A hub is connected when its controller holds its capital and a path of rail it controls and has converted joins them. The connection's level is the path's weakest link, taking the best path (Germany 1936: 26 of 29 hubs connected, 14 at level 1, 6 at 2, 6 at 3; East Prussia is cut off by the Polish corridor)
- **Capacity** - A connected hub: 10 + 5 per connection level (`RAILWAY_BASE_FLOW`, `RAILWAY_FLOW_PER_LEVEL`). The capital: 5 + 0.3 per civilian factory + 0.6 per military factory + 0.4 per dockyard of the states working for the country
- **When** - Daily, first in the day, only when control, ownership, buildings or a finishing conversion changed something (`railways` column in perf.tsv)
- **On the map** - `Hoi4RailRenderer`: every rail link as HOI4's track texture for its level (`gfx/models/supply/railroad.dds`, one tile per level, rusty to steel), 0.7 map px wide, through each province's `supply_node` spot as a centripetal Catmull-Rom curve, chained through plain pass-through provinces. Shown from the zoom where unit counters appear (fade 3 to 5 screen px per map px). Rail still converting gets a grey strip on top. Drawn with the engine's `MapLineLayer`; prototype `Tools/prototypes/supply/render_rails.py`, scale checked against an HOI4 screenshot. No bridges, hub icons or supply map mode yet
- **Recording** - `hubs.tsv`: every hub at the start, then a row when its controller, connection, level, distance (rail links from the capital) or capacity changes
- **Hub supply** - `Hoi4Supply`: every connected hub sends supply over the land provinces its controller controls, starting at 5 for the capital and 2.8 for a hub (+0.34 per rail level) and losing on each step 0.5 + 1.2 (capital) or 0.7 (hub) times the defines' logistics curve, plus the terrain's `supply_flow_penalty_factor` (forest 0.08 to marsh 0.16, urban -0.2), cut by up to 30% by the infrastructure of the state it leaves, +0.2 over a river. A province's hub supply is the sum of its best three sources. Our reading of the defines, fitted in `Tools/prototypes/supply/supply_flow.py` to HOI4 tooltips in the Ruhr (Dortmund hub 7.06 against 7.46; light rain in the game, none here)
- **State supply** - A pool per state: 0.3 per infrastructure level, 0.18 per million people, 0.2 + 0.05 x value per victory point, `local_supplies` x 0.2, rounded to 0.1 (matches HOI4's tooltips exactly: Westfalen 2.60, Rhineland 3.50). A state split between controllers splits all but the VPs by provinces held
- **Division supply** - Each division needs its template's supply use (sum of its sub-units' `supply_consumption`; HOI4's designer shows about 14% more, a modifier not found yet). Daily: the state pool is shared evenly by the controller's divisions in the state, then each province's remaining need draws on its hub supply, split over its sources by flow; a hub asked for more than its capacity scales everyone down. A division's ratio climbs toward what it gets by at most 3 points a day and falls a quarter of the way a day (at least 1 point); both measured in a real HOI4 game (rises median +1.9, steepest tenth +4.7; drops median -0.8, steepest tenth -8.7). Prototype `supply/supply_demand.py`: peacetime 1936 armies fully supplied, 33 divisions stacked in Berlin get 61%
- **Low supply** - A division's lack is 1 - its ratio, reaching full weight after 30 days in a row below 99% (`MAX_OUT_OF_SUPPLY_DAYS`, `LOW_SUPPLY`). At full lack: attacking, -25% attack and -65% defence and breakthrough; defending, -35% attack and -15% defence (`COMBAT_SUPPLY_LACK_*`); organisation capped from half supply down, to 35% of max at none (`SUPPLY_ORG_MAX_CAP`; the half-supply start measured: real divisions after 20+ days at 10-19% supply hold at most 0.60 of max org, at 20-29% 0.68, from 40% about all; above the cap it drains) and recovering 20% slower (`OUT_OF_SUPPLY_MORALE`); 80% slower (`OUT_OF_SUPPLY_SPEED`); manpower reinforcement down to 10% (ours; the wiki: low supply slows men, not equipment). Below 35% supply (`SUPPLY_THRESHOLD_FOR_ARMY_ATTRITION`) it loses equipment daily, up to 0.15% a day at no supply for a division of average reliability (measured in a real HOI4 game: -0.12% a day at 0-9% supply; `ATTRITION_EQUIPMENT_LOSS_CHANCE` read literally gives 20 times that). Checked with `meme_ger`'s daily division snapshots (94,880 division-days); the combat penalties and the 30-day ramp are the defines, unchecked
- **Supply map mode** - F4 (F1 back to the political map; `Hoi4MapModeKeys`). `Hoi4SupplyMapMode` colours the player's and allies' land by its supply (hub supply + state pool) over 12 on HOI4's `SUPPLY_MAP_MODE_REACH_COLOR` gradient (purple at none, dark blue, blue, light blue, teal, light green), or, where the player's divisions average under 90% supply, by their status (`SUPPLY_MAP_MODE_STATUS_COLOR`: red at none, orange, mustard); the rest is plain terrain. The shader (shader mode 6) smooths the colours across provinces over 5 map px and stops them at the country's edge (`_Hoi4SupplyMap` in the visual style). Railways switch to HOI4's white supply-mode map arrows (`gfx/maparrows/railway_*`: thin for level 1, medium 2-3, thick 4-5), wider with each level and shown at every zoom. Recoloured daily while shown. Prototype `Tools/prototypes/supply/supply_mapmode.py`
- **Low supply crates** - The player's counters show HOI4's supply crate (`supply_strip.dds`, mapicons.gui `low_supply`: over the top edge, 0.75 scale) for the lowest-supplied division in the stack: yellow under 99%, orange under 50%, red under 20% (`LOW_SUPPLY`, `ALERT_LOW_SUPPLY_LEVEL`, `ALERT_VERY_LOW_SUPPLY_LEVEL`)
- **Supply recording** - `supply.tsv`: per country weekly, divisions, need, supply from states and hubs, divisions full and under half, mean ratio. `division_supply.tsv`: a division's ratio, target and the province's supply whenever the ratio enters another 10% band. `supply` column in perf.tsv

## Weather

`Hoi4Weather` (`Assets/Game/Scripts/Weather/`), daily before supply. Data: each strategic region's weather periods (`map/hoi4_weather.json5`, from `map/strategicregions`); rules: `common/weather.txt` and the wiki's Weather page. Prototype `Tools/prototypes/weather/weather.py` (its 1936: Pomerania snowed in by February, mud after the thaw, Ukraine's spring and autumn mud, northern France's wet winters).

- **Weather events** - One per strategic region (none, light rain, heavy rain, snow, blizzard, sandstorm), picked by the month's weights and lasting `weather.txt`'s durations (the smaller of two rolls). Ours: one roll per region; HOI4 drifts weather systems across regions
- **Temperature** - Per province, inside the month's range plus the terrain's bias (mountain -7, hills -3, forest -1, urban +3, jungle +5): up to 2 degrees of noise a day and half the way to the region's drifting target, at most 6, then 30% toward its neighbours. Bands: extremely cold (-12 and under), very cold (to -4), very hot (20 to 28), extremely hot (28+)
- **Snow** - Snowing at 1 degree or colder, full from -4: +1 a day (blizzard +5), mountains +80%, cities -40%, deserts none, up to 80. 20-40 ground snow, 40+ deep snow. Melts from 0 degrees (0.1% to 90% a day at 12) into collected water
- **Mud** - Rain collects water (light 0.5, heavy 1.8 a day, times the month's mud factor, plains +50%, marsh +90%, mountains -30%, -80% at infrastructure 5), drying from 2 degrees (0.05% to 40% a day at 38; a quarter of that on rainy days, ours: else cool wet summers stayed mud). Mud from 15 water until under 13.5 (ours, from `chance_increase`), frozen at -1 or colder
- **Effects** (`00_static_modifiers.txt`) - Hub supply into a province: snow -10%, blizzard -25%, heavy rain -5%, sandstorm -15%, ground snow -20%, deep -40%, mud -15%, extremely cold -15%, very hot -5% (state supply untouched). Supply use: heavy rain +10%, blizzard +15%, extremely hot +50%. Speed into a province: ground snow -30%, deep -70%, mud -50%. Attack in a battle there (both sides): ground snow -20%, deep -40%, mud -40%, extremely cold -20%, very cold -10%, extremely hot -10%. Attrition at war: mud 0.7, winter 0.4 / 0.25, heat 0.1 / 0.2, on low supply's scale (0.2 = its full attrition)
- **Snow on the map** - HOI4's ApplySnow ported (`Hoi4Weather.hlsl`, fed by `Hoi4WeatherMap`): each province's snow in a province-indexed texture, blended across province edges and faded between days over 1.5 s. A frost wash from the first snow, patchy cover through HOI4's snow noise, full cover on flat ground in deep snow; none on cliffs or cities (the atlas's specular). Less opaque zoomed out. HOI4's snow texture and SNOW_COLOR; `_Hoi4Snow` sets the amount that reads as full snow (40, not in HOI4's shaders). Prototype `weather/snow_render.py`
- **Mud on the map** - HOI4's GetMudColor after the snow: muddy provinces take HOI4's mud texture (overlaid by the colormap, its normal, gloss and specular), broken up by noise zoomed out. `_Hoi4Mud` sets how strongly (0.75; at 1, HOI4's formula, the ground turns solid brown)
- **Ice on the water** - HOI4's ApplyIce: water near snowy land freezes into broken pack ice (HOI4's ice texture and noise), out to about 6 map px from the smooth coastline (`_Hoi4Ice`; HOI4 bleeds land snow onto the water through its filtered snow texture, ours fades the snowiest nearby land's snow over a distance-from-coast field in the terrain index alpha, so the edge follows the coast with a ragged fringe, not province pixels), so small lakes freeze over. `_Hoi4IceLook`: cover, brightness (ours 1.5) and falloff; the ice is not darkened like the water. Only on the northern quarter of the map, as in HOI4; fades out zoomed out (later than HOI4, which hides it close in)
- **Weather clouds** - `Hoi4WeatherSky` paints the engine atmosphere's cloud mask from each province's weather (blurred, cross-faded over 2 s): wispy, billowy clouds (the mask read through a noise warp): a dark mass with a heavy ground shadow over thunderstorms, white clouds over blizzards, grey over rain and snow, sand over sandstorms; high up with the normal clouds, from fully zoomed out until unit level (`maskZoom`). Prototype `weather/weather_clouds.py`, checked against HOI4 screenshots
- **Falling weather** - `Hoi4/Precipitation`, procedural on one map quad when zoomed in, anchored to the map around the screen centre but with screen axes, so it falls across the screen from the top left to the bottom right like HOI4's and pans and zooms with the map: thin rain streaks (heavy rain denser), drifting snowflakes (blizzard denser), the odd lightning flash over thunderstorms (a cloud-shaped patch of the storm lighting up). Fog where it rains, in the same zoom range (`Hoi4Fog.hlsl`, shared by the terrain shader and the overlay): the ground loses its colour, a pale haze with lighter puffs drifting in it lies over everything, greyer over thunderstorms. Off for now (`_Hoi4Fog` all zero; tuned values in the visual style's comment). `_Hoi4Fog`: desaturation, haze, puffs, puff size. `_Hoi4Precip`: opacity, zoom range, density
- **Frozen rivers** - HOI4's ApplyRiverSnow in `Hoi4/RiverMesh` (the engine's line pass with a GAME fragment, named by the visual style's rivers section): where the land is snowy the river turns to pale ice with snowy banks, through HOI4's snow noise, less zoomed out
- **Shown** - The supply map mode's tooltip: temperature, weather, snow, mud and the hub supply cut. `weather.tsv`: weekly per strategic region, the event, mean temperature and the share under snow, deep snow and mud; `weather` column in perf.tsv

## AI

- **War declarations (stress test)** - Off by default, F8 toggles it. Every 3 days each AI country with at least 2 divisions and fewer than 2 wars it can reach (an enemy whose land doesn't touch its own, straits included, doesn't count, so one left on an island doesn't block it) declares war on its weakest land neighbour, if that neighbour has at most 110% of its division strength (not allies, not the player unless set in the inspector). At most 5 new wars per check. Settings on the Hoi4AiWars object

- **Front lines** - Each AI country at war gets an (undrawn) front line per enemy it borders, holding all its idle divisions
- **War AI: theatres and armies** (country layer, Docs/ai-layers.md) - Planned weekly per AI country at its own hour, early when the picture changes (no plan yet, other enemies, the front a quarter longer or shorter, an army gone, or over a tenth of the front beyond every army's section); on the other days its armies' sections follow the front by nearness and the lines, divisions and posture are updated from the plan. A plan: its fronts against every enemy grouped into theatres (connected stretches, across enemies; stretches within 4 land hops are one theatre, a sliver under 4 provinces joins one up to 8 away). Each landmass (land it may walk on that no enemy controls; enemy divisions standing in it don't split it; no naval transfer yet) shares its own divisions among its own theatres with its own main effort, so land across the sea and encircled pockets are budgeted from the divisions there. Per theatre what it takes: enough to fill the line (one division per front province) and to match the enemy divisions facing it (within 2 hops) times 0.5 to 1.5 by the danger to the homeland (the enemy's cheapest route from there to the country's core victory points: terrain and rivers over land it controls), whichever is more, the main effort 1.5x the enemy; a front near home also scores up to twice as high as a place to attack; and a score from the cheapest route on to an enemy's victory points; the best is the main effort, committed 60 days unless another scores 1.5x. Budgets: each theatre its need, the spare shared in proportion to the needs, or when short each the same fraction of its need; capped by supply: a theatre takes at most what its front provinces feed (their hub supply and their states' pools over the country's average division need, as HOI4's `AI_FRONT_DIVISIONS_PER_SUPPLY_POINT`; at least one per front province), the rest goes to theatres with room, then to reserve. Reserve divisions starving where they stand (under 60% of their need) walk daily to the nearest of the country's provinces off the front whose hub supply has room for them (within 30 hops, up to 24 orders a day; `ai_country/rest` notes). Posture: the main effort attacks while its armies have 1.5x the enemy facing it; every other front holds, attacking only bulges into its line (a target next to at least two of its provinces) at 2:1 odds or empty. Each theatre gets ceil(budget / 24) armies (HOI4's army size), each holding a contiguous section of its front: sections carry over day to day by nearness, armies merge or split to fit, and whole armies move to another theatre within 1500 km (else they are disbanded where they stand). Each army holds its section with the player's kind of front line, one per enemy it faces. A theatre more than a quarter over its budget (at least 2) lets the furthest divisions of its most over-strength armies go to reserve (uneven armies on one line are left alone); armies under it take the nearest reserve divisions they can walk to (within 20 hops, or further only when below half their budget). Decisions are noted (system ai_country) and in report.py's War AI section. Menu HOI4 > Show AI Plans draws the AI's lines and army colours
- **Attacks** - Once a day at its own hour (country id mod 24, so the work is spread over the day), each AI country attacks the border provinces of every enemy it borders target by target: the main effort's first, cheapest route on to the enemy's victory points first, then the rest (least loaded, least defended first). Each target gathers the idle, rested divisions next to it, so attacks come only from the line, battle-plan style (the line attacks across itself and moves forward as a whole; gathering from 3-5 hops away left holes and stray groups), until the estimated odds are good (`Hoi4Combat.Odds`: hits from what fits the combat width, worn against every live division's organisation, reserves included), and the group attacks together, with up to three times the width as reserves behind the front so a narrow front is ground down; a group short of good odds takes the uneven fight now and then on the main effort (never elsewhere), never a hopeless one, and otherwise stays free for the next target. A division next to its target goes straight in; at most 12 path searches per country per day for those further back
- **Encirclement** - AI land cut off from the piece with the capital and surrounded by enemies (no coast, no neutral or allied neighbour: held land across the sea isn't a pocket) is a pocket: its divisions stay out of the line's attacks and break out across the pocket's border province nearest the main body (land hops), at any odds but hopeless ones, holding otherwise
- **Calling off** - Hourly, attacks that go badly are called off before the attackers are ground down; attackers rejoin their line afterwards
- **Deterministic dice** - Per country, province and day, so the same game makes the same choices
- **Same commands as the player** - AI orders go through the same commands, so combat, retreat and occupation apply unchanged

## Simulation

- **Commands** - Every player or AI decision (move, halt, leave plan, create army, add to army, place / delete battle plan, place offensive, execute plan, borrow for attack) goes through `gameState.TryExecuteCommand`: validated, logged, replayable, network-ready
- **Fixed-order tick** - `Hoi4Simulation` runs combat, then battle plans, then AI break-offs every hour, and plan checks plus new AI attacks every day
- **Determinism** - `FixedPoint64` maths, loops in id order, adjacency-based fronts, AI decides on the host only (Archon lockstep model)
- **Session recordings** - Every editor play session is recorded to `Recordings/<stamp>_<TAG>/` (newest 12 kept): divisions, plans, plan fronts, battles, control changes, commands, decision notes, log, and `perf.tsv` (milliseconds per simulation system per game day, frame times, wars, divisions). Read with `Tools/recorder/report.py`, `unit.py`, `battles.py` and the engine's `rows.py`. Menu **HOI4 > Record Sessions** turns recording off (remembered per machine), for clean profiling: it costs about 15 ms per game day in a large war
- **Performance for large wars** - Built on the AI war stress test: one shared controller / owner snapshot per change of control (not one read per system per hour), flat state and region lookups, target-first AI attacks with a path-search budget, cheaper movement rules and counters. Each simulation step has a Unity profiler marker named like its `perf.tsv` column (`Hoi4.<step>`), visible in a normal capture
- **Scene guard** - Game registrations only run in HOI4 scenes (`Hoi4Scenes.Active`), so the engine's StarterKit scene stays clean. The StarterKit demo systems (economy, buildings, AI, province history) are switched off in `Hoi4Test`

## UI

All panels follow one pattern: structure in `.uxml`, look in `*Style.uss` + `Hoi4Theme.uss`, display logic in a static Presenter, a thin View MonoBehaviour that wires events. Sprites are named in USS (`--sprite: "gfx/..."`) and loaded at runtime from the data directory, so no HOI4 art is in the project or a build.

- **Top bar** - Player flag, political power (+2/day), stability, war support, live manpower pool, date pill (click or Space to pause), speed down/up (-/+), five speed steps. Shown once a country is picked
- **State panel** - Click a land province to see its state: owner, cores, manpower, resources, victory points, buildings, categories. Appears only after a country is selected; hidden while divisions are selected; Escape hides it
- **Division panel** - Selected divisions (widened to the whole army) as cards with organisation, strength and planning bars, HOI4's entrenchment shovel and bar, icon, status, per-card close, select-half, scrollable list
- **Army cards and battle plan window** - See Armies and Battle Plans
- **Diplomacy panel** - Right-click a country's land (with no divisions selected): country, relation, Declare War / Make Peace. Opens the diplomatic map mode; X or Escape closes it and restores the previous mode. Declaring war ends guarantees and military access between the two
- **Scroll capture** - The mouse wheel scrolls panels instead of zooming the map while the pointer is over one
- **Production screen** - See Production
- **HOI4 font** - Oswald, applied to all panels

## Data Pipeline

`python Tools/hoi4_convert.py [--hoi4 "<install>"] [--out Hoi4Data]` (needs numpy, scipy, Pillow)

- **Provinces, definitions, terrain, heightmap, rivers** - Archon-format data directory
- **Strait crossings** - HOI4 `map/adjacencies.csv`: `sea` links between two land provinces and `impassable` borders go to `map/adjacencies.csv` (sea-to-sea canal and strait rules are left out, no navy)
- **States and strategic regions** - `map/hoi4_regions.json5` (states, regions, impassable states, capitals, water)
- **Combat terrain** - Combat width, support width and attack penalty per terrain in `map/terrain.json5`; river crossings between provinces in `map/hoi4_river_crossings.json5`
- **State details** - `map/hoi4_state_data.json5`
- **Railways and supply hubs** - `map/hoi4_railways.json5`: rail links `[a, b, level]`, hubs and each province's rail point (its `supply_node` spot in `map/buildings.txt`), from `map/railways.txt` and `map/supply_nodes.txt`; the track tiles as `gfx/map/rails/rail_<level>.png`
- **Countries** - Colors, ideologies, stats (`common/hoi4_country_stats.json5`: stability, war support, core and non-core population, mobilisation law), history
- **Localisation** - Names for provinces, states, countries
- **Order of battle** - 1936 divisions
- **Terrain and water textures** - Atlases, colormaps, water cubemap, normals, border strips
- **Interface sprites** - Top bar, unit panel, state view, counters and action icons, move arrow, battle plan arrows and lines, army cards, flags
- **Own Paradox-script parser** - `parse_pdx`
- `Hoi4Data/` is gitignored and derived from the user's install; never commit or redistribute it

## Known Gaps

- Combat ignores forts, generals, night, stacking and per-unit terrain modifiers (only the terrain's base penalty applies); max entrenchment ignores the division's own entrenchment stat (engineers)
- Offensives are broad-front only; no spearhead or encirclement logic
- AI does not use offensives or armies, only odds-based border attacks
- The division designer has no division equipment window, name list choice, 3D model, terrain adjusters or army experience (costs are shown, not paid); sub-unit availability is 1936's (no research)
- Recruit and deploy has no equipment priorities, no training experience or exercises, no special forces cap, and deployment orders aren't saved
- Construction has no trade, repairs, conversion, province buildings (forts, naval bases, railways), slot bonuses from technology or law changes
- Production has no strategic resources or trade, no factory construction, no factory output modifiers (real Germany makes about 17% more per factory), no civilian factories; air and naval lines are not converted, so their factories end up free (the AI puts them on land equipment)
- Equipment designs and variants are not modelled: an archetype is one kind of equipment, stats from the 1936 design
- No recruiting new divisions; manpower only reinforces
- Province selection does not persist after the state panel closes (HOI4 keeps it)
- Only land units exist; no air or naval
- Weather: no weather sounds, the falling weather is procedural (not HOI4's particles and textures), no acclimatisation, no organisation effects (regain, loss when moving), no breakthrough or dig-in effects, no flooding or arctic water, not saved (rebuilt from the start)
- Supply: no attrition to organisation or from moving, no ports, convoys or air supply (hubs only connect by rail, overseas divisions starve), no allies' hubs, no supply map hover (hub routes, tooltips beyond the province's numbers), rivers don't count as level-1 links, no allied rail, no trains or motorisation, no railway or hub construction, and a lost capital doesn't move (its whole network is cut)
- Political power only counts up; nothing spends it
- Peace conferences are one turn with Take State only: no puppets, liberation, government change, contests, compliance or war goals; war participation ignores air and naval and the attacker-and-winner bonus
- Plan confidence indicator on army cards is always the tick
